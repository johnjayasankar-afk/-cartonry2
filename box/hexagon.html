Hexagonal box

A six-panel box that reads as considered rather than shipped. Candles, cosmetics, gifts and anything that wants to look like it was chosen.

Open this style in the generator → Free to preview at your own dimensions.

When to use it

When the shape is doing marketing work. A hexagon stands up well, groups neatly on a shelf, and photographs far better than a cube.

How it is put together

Six equal panels wrap into a hexagonal tube and glue at a flap. Bottom flaps fold in one after another and overlap; glue or tape them in sequence. Length is measured across the flats, which is how you would measure the finished box with a ruler.

What to watch for

A hexagon turns only 60 degrees at each corner, not 90, so it needs a smaller board allowance than a square box — 0.577 of a caliper across each panel rather than a full one. Add a whole caliper the way you would for a square box and the finished hexagon comes out oversized and slack.

How it assembles

  1. Score all six creases and the flap fold.
  2. Wrap into a hexagonal tube and glue the flap to the sixth panel.
  3. Fold the bottom flaps in one after another, each overlapping the last.
  4. Glue or tape the final flap under the first to close the base.

Four-corner tray

A base with four walls folded up and tabs locking the corners. The simplest way to turn a flat sheet into something that holds things.

Open this style in the generator → Free to preview at your own dimensions.

When to use it

Inserts and inners, produce and bakery trays, the base of a two-piece box, or a shelf-ready display. Also the right starting point when you want a tray plus a separate lid.

How it is put together

The blank is a cross. The base is in the middle, the four walls fold up from it, and a tab on each end of the front and back walls swings round to sit behind the side walls, where it is glued or tucked.

What to watch for

Wall height cannot exceed half the shorter base dimension, or opposite corner tabs collide across the blank. Note also that a wall folded at one end and cut at the other only needs half a caliper of allowance, not a whole one — a detail that quietly makes hand-drawn trays a millimetre or two too deep.

How it assembles

  1. Score every dashed line.
  2. Fold the two side walls up from the base.
  3. Fold the front and back walls up, swinging their corner tabs in behind the side walls.
  4. Glue or tuck the tabs against the side walls to lock the corners.

Tuck-top mailer box

The box that made subscription boxes look good: a hinged lid at the back, a tuck flap at the front, and walls that fold up without a drop of glue.

Open this style in the generator → Free to preview at your own dimensions.

When to use it

Direct-to-consumer shipping, subscription boxes, anything where the customer opening the box is part of the product. It ships flat and folds by hand in seconds, so it suits packing by people rather than machines.

How it is put together

The base carries four walls. The side walls fold up first; the front and back walls follow, and their corner tabs swing in behind the sides to lock the corners. The lid hinges off the back wall, its own dust flaps drop inside the side walls, and a tuck flap at the front holds it shut.

What to watch for

Depth is the constraint. Once the depth passes half the width, the lid dust flaps are longer than the box is wide and foul the side walls — the generator will tell you before you cut a die. Keep it shallower than that and it folds cleanly.

How it assembles

  1. Score every dashed line. No glue is needed anywhere.
  2. Fold the two side walls up from the base.
  3. Fold the front wall up and swing its corner tabs in behind the side walls.
  4. Do the same at the back, then fold the lid over on its hinge.
  5. Drop the lid's dust flaps inside the side walls and tuck the front flap into the front wall.

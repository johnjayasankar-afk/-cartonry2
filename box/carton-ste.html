Straight Tuck End carton (STE)

The retail folding carton, with both tuck flaps hinged from the back panel so the front face is clean and unbroken.

Open this style in the generator → Free to preview at your own dimensions.

When to use it

Anything that sits on a shelf face-out — cosmetics, supplements, confectionery, small electronics. Choose straight tuck over reverse tuck when the front of the pack has to look perfect.

How it is put together

Four panels wrap into a tube with a glue flap. At each end the back panel carries a tuck panel that spans the opening and then a narrower tuck flap that slips inside the front panel. The side panels carry dust flaps that fold in first and close the corners.

What to watch for

Both tucks hinging from the same panel means the blank nests less efficiently on the sheet than the reverse tuck version, so it costs a little more board. That is the price of the clean front, and usually worth paying for a retail pack.

How it assembles

  1. Score every dashed line and fold the glue flap.
  2. Form the tube and glue the flap to the far panel - this is how it arrives from the converter, flat.
  3. Square the carton. At the bottom, fold the two dust flaps in, bring the tuck panel over them, and slip its tuck flap inside the front panel.
  4. Fill, then close the top the same way.

Other box styles

  • Reverse Tuck End carton (RTE) Retail folding carton. Tucks hinge from opposite panels, nesting tighter on the sheet.
  • Regular Slotted Container (RSC) The standard shipping box. All flaps the same depth; outer flaps meet at the centre.
  • Half Slotted Container (open top) An RSC with no top flaps - an open tray for a separate lid to telescope over.
  • Full Overlap Slotted Container (FOL) Flaps run the full panel width and fully overlap - a stronger, stiffer base for heavy goods.

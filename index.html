      How it assembles

        What this makes

        A dieline is the flat cutting template a box is made from — the outline the knife follows and the lines the board is creased along. Cartonry computes one for the internal size you type, with the board thickness already allowed for, and hands you a file you can put artwork onto or send to a die maker.

        Every panel is derived from the dimensions you enter. Panels across the blank are set to the internal dimension plus one board caliper, because folding a sheet through ninety degrees consumes one thickness of inner face at each corner. Get that wrong and the box is a few millimetres too tight to close.

        Box styles

        • Regular Slotted Container (RSC) 0201 Free The standard shipping box. All flaps the same depth; outer flaps meet at the centre.
        • Half Slotted Container (open top) An RSC with no top flaps - an open tray for a separate lid to telescope over.
        • Full Overlap Slotted Container (FOL) 0203 Flaps run the full panel width and fully overlap - a stronger, stiffer base for heavy goods.
        • Tuck-top mailer box The e-commerce mailer: hinged lid at the back, tuck flap at the front, four-corner walls.
        • Four-corner tray Open tray with glued or tucked corner tabs - inserts, produce trays, box bases.
        • Telescope lid A lid sized to slide over a box you already have. Enter the box it must cover, not the lid.
        • Straight Tuck End carton (STE) Retail folding carton. Both tucks hinge from the back panel for a clean front face.
        • Reverse Tuck End carton (RTE) Retail folding carton. Tucks hinge from opposite panels, nesting tighter on the sheet.
        • Sleeve / belly band Free An open-ended band that slides over a tray or product. The simplest print-and-glue format.
        • Hexagonal box Six-sided box for candles, gifts and cosmetics. Length is measured across the flats.
        • Pillow box Curved-end pillow pack for gifts, jewellery and small retail. No end flaps - the arcs close it.

        Each style has a reference page covering when to use it, how it is put together, and what people get wrong.

        Help

        Which dimensions do I enter?

        Always the internal size — the space your product needs. Cartonry adds the board allowances on top. If you enter the outside size, the box will be smaller inside than you expect.

        What are the coloured lines?

        Magenta solid is the cut. Blue dashed is a crease (fold). Green dashed marks the glue area. They come through as separate named layers in SVG and DXF so your printer or die maker can separate them.

        Which file should I send?

        PDF for a printer or for placing artwork — it is written at true scale, so one millimetre in the file is one millimetre on paper. DXF for a die maker or a cutting table (R12, millimetres, layered). SVG to open in Illustrator, Affinity, Inkscape or CorelDRAW. Spec sheet is a one-page A4 summary — the drawing plus every number a converter will ask for — meant to be attached to the email rather than measured. It carries no pricing: what your board costs is your business, not your supplier's, so it stays on screen.

        What is the 3D fold for?

        It folds the flat blank in front of you so you can see the box before you commit to anything. It is not a picture: every panel is the same geometry the dieline is cut from, and our tests fold the net and measure the assembled box against the dimensions you typed. The pillow box has no 3D view because it closes by bending rather than folding, and an approximation would look convincing and be wrong.

        Can I work in inches?

        Yes — press the in toggle, or U. Everything you measure follows: the box, the blank, the panel sizes, the sheet, the gutter and trim, the written notes, the print dialog and the spec sheet. Board area switches with it, square metres becoming square feet, and if you have entered a board price it is converted so the cost per box stays the same money. You can type fractions the way you would say them — 12 1/2 and 12-1/2 both work.

        Board caliper and slot width stay in millimetres in both modes. That is deliberate, not an oversight: board is specified in millimetres or microns by every mill and converter, in the United States as much as anywhere, and 0.0138 in is not a figure your supplier would recognise. Grammage stays g/m² for the same reason.

        My box is bigger than my printer

        Almost every box is. A 200 × 150 × 100 mm shipping case unfolds to a blank 747 mm across — larger than A2. Press Print at home and the dieline is split across sheets of your own paper at exactly 1:1, with a trim line on each sheet and a sheet map to lay them out by. Cut along the long grey dashes, butt the cut edges together and tape the back. Every sheet carries a printed 100 mm bar: measure it before you cut, because a print dialog left on “fit to page” will shrink the whole thing by a few per cent and nothing will fit.

        Can I send someone this exact box?

        Yes — press Copy link. The whole specification lives in the address, so the link reopens the same style at the same dimensions on the same board. Press ? for the other keyboard shortcuts.

        Does my design leave my computer?

        No. Every dieline is computed in your browser and the files are generated there too. Nothing you type is uploaded, because there is no server to upload it to. The only network request this site can make is a licence check, which sends your licence key and nothing else. Once loaded, the tool keeps working with no connection at all.

        Check before you commit to a die

        Cartonry is a drafting tool, not a substitute for your converter's approval. Print the PDF at 100%, fold it, and check it against the product before you order tooling. Converters vary in the allowances they prefer, and the advanced panel lets you match theirs.
